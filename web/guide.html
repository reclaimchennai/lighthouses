The map

Lights
Every active light in DGLL's Master Ledgers, plus the Perigee light vessel and three public-access lighthouses DGLL publishes no ledger for (Kannur's ledger is filed on DGLL's Mahe page; Kelshi, Rawal Pir and Mahe come from the Lighthouse Directory). Each flashes its ledger rhythm.
Beams
Revolving optics sweep 360° at the ledger rotation speed, with the ledger's beam count and width. LED flashers and fixed drum optics blink in place. A shaft leaves the lantern and hands over to the sweep on the water.
Over land
A lighthouse lights the land behind it too, so the sweep crosses the shore and towns, fainter (nothing reflects it back, and buildings and trees block it low down). It goes dark only where a ledger records a screen: 8 lanterns have panes blanked on the land side, and 2 lights state a narrower visibility sector.
Reach
The lit sea around each light: the shorter of luminous and geographical range, cast as sight lines that stop at the first landfall (headlands and islands shadow the beam).
NAVTEX
Wavefronts over the 250 NM DGLL states for its seven transmitters, on connected sea only. They brighten during each station's real 10-minute UTC broadcast slot. Hidden before the year the ledgers record NAVTEX equipment.
RACON
Radar beacons pulse their Morse identifier outward. On by default, fading out close up. Hidden before their installation year.
Navigational warnings
Every warning India's National Hydrographic Office has in force, read from its India WINS map every two hours: NAVTEX and NAVAREA VIII warnings (on by default) and Temporary & Preliminary Notices to Mariners (off). Danger areas are hatched and point warnings are dots, coloured by hazard: firing or danger area, survey / rig / cable work, wreck or danger, light / buoy / RACON, notice. Tap one for its full text. When a warning reports a light unlit or a RACON off, that light goes dark or loses its Morse rings on the map, and its card says so. Warnings show only at today's year on the History slider.
3D buildings
Close up (zoom 14 and nearer), OpenStreetMap buildings rise to their mapped height.
Moving around
+ and − zoom. The 2D / 3D button flattens the map or tilts it back. Drag to pan; right-drag (or two fingers) to rotate and tilt; Ctrl + drag to roll. Keyboard: arrows pan, + and − zoom, Shift + arrows rotate and tilt.
Opening a light
Tap a light or its name: the camera flies out to sea and turns to face the tower, and the card opens beside it (below it on a phone).
3D towers
Built from each ledger's tower type, height and colours; the light vessel from DGLL's photograph. Towers are drawn taller than life when zoomed out so they stay visible.
Places, roads & rivers
OpenStreetMap data via OpenFreeMap, loaded after the lights. India's outline and state lines are DataMeet's Survey of India composite.
Planned rings
Four lighthouses ordered on the Brahmaputra (National Waterway-2). Rings mark the named river ports; exact sites are not published.
Year watermark
Top left. It follows the History slider and is burned into pictures and videos.

Panel

Show
Switch each layer on or off. "On air" names the NAVTEX station broadcasting right now.
Navigational warnings
How many NAVTEX and NAVAREA VIII warnings are in force and when they were last checked, switches for them and for T&P notices, a key with counts per hazard, and the warning archive: every warning in force and every one that has ended since the archive began (seeded with NHO's lists in force on 7 July 2025), searchable by place, text and number.
Colour lights by
Light: the ledger light colour. Equipment: NAVTEX, RACON or light only. Age: year established. Access: museum, public access (Lok Sabha Q3233) or none.
History
Drag or play from 1790 to today. Lights appear from their establishment year; NAVTEX and RACON from their equipment years. Coloured by Age, Play slows down and the camera flies to each light as it is lit, with the year and name under the watermark (and in any video you record).
Fly to
Below History: the west and east coasts, Lakshadweep, Andaman & Nicobar, Chennai and the light vessel. Night voyage tours the coast with lights only, switching the NAVTEX and RACON radio aids on off Mangalore.
Public access
The 75 lighthouses the government lists as developed for visitors, and the 7 with museums.
Funding
DGLL's allocation and spending on lighthouse infrastructure, 2023-24 to 2025-26 (Lok Sabha Q949). Hover a year for exact figures.
Rhythm
Every light's flashes in one scrolling wall, one row per light along the coast. Tap a row to go to that light.
Picture & video
The camera button takes a picture; the record button records up to 90 seconds as MP4 (WebM where the browser has no MP4 encoder). Both carry the lighthouse names, the year, the credits and a legend of exactly what is in the scene: a legend row appears when its layer comes into view (NAVTEX switching on mid-voyage, buildings rising close up) and fades when it leaves. The result opens in a viewer first, to look at, save or share.

Lighthouse card

Tap any light. Values come from DGLL's Master Ledger unless marked † (computed from other ledger values, or a network year where the station's ledger gives none) or ‡ (from the Lighthouse Directory). Each mark is explained under its section.

Light

Characteristic
The light's signature in one wording for every light: the international abbreviation, then plain words (Fl(2) W 10s · White, 2 flashes every 10 s). Hover for the ledger's own text.
Period
Seconds for one full cycle of the signature.
Sequence
Seconds lit and dark within a period. Printed timings where the ledger gives them; otherwise built from the characteristic and flash duration.
Optic
Whether the lens revolves, flashes in place, or the ledger doesn't say.
Rotation
Seconds per turn, from the optic rpm when it fits the rhythm, else from the panel count.
Beams
Number of beams and their width: the ledger's horizontal divergence where printed.
Lens panels
Panels that shine; blanked panels are counted out ("4 lit of 6").
Sectors
White/red sector lights. The ledgers rarely give the sector bearings, so these draw white.

Brightness, reach, station

Brightness
Effective beam intensity in candela, on a log scale against every DGLL light that states one, a candle (≈1 cd) and one car high-beam lamp (140,000 cd, the ECE maximum per lamp). Lighthouse candela sit near a headlamp's because a light at sea only has to reach a sailor's eye across a dark sea. The card also runs the IALA formula (night, 10 NM visibility) to show how far the stated intensity carries, and flags ledgers whose range and intensity disagree by more than 30%.
Reach
Luminous range (how far the intensity carries in clear weather) and geographical range (the horizon from the lantern's height to a 5 m eye). The light is seen out to the shorter one.
Equipment pills
NAVTEX with its transmitter IDs, RACON with its Morse letter, AIS base station, DGNSS, VTS radar, museum, public access, solar power.
Station
Tower type, height and colours; established and tower years; Admiralty List of Lights and ARLHS numbers; NAVTEX broadcast slots.
Warnings
Amber boxes flag where a ledger contradicts itself, e.g. an rpm that cannot produce the printed rhythm.
Transcription notes
How an awkward field was read: formats, blanks, misfiled or copied pages.
Tenders
Every DGLL tender that names the station, newest first, with the work at this site and the estimated cost. Each opens in the tender archive.
Photo
DGLL's station photo (or Wikimedia Commons), served from this site's own backup. Tap it: the viewer opens at once on a light, legible copy; "Full resolution" loads the untouched original, with its size shown first. Pinch, scroll or +/− to zoom, drag to pan.
3D scan
Where a lighthouse has been scanned (a Gaussian splat built from many photos of it), a 3D scan button opens it to orbit and zoom. Every photo used and its licence are listed with the scan.

Tender archive

Tender archive lists every tender on DGLL's current and archive tender pages. DGLL takes notices down over time, so every notice and corrigendum is kept here unaltered, with its SHA-256.

Summary
What is being bought or built, and where, in one or two sentences.
Sites
The stations named in the documents, linked to the map, with the work at each.
Key facts
Procurement mode and portal, reference numbers, estimated cost, EMD, fees, security, completion period and dates.
Scope
Items, quantities and units from the notice or bill of quantities.
Specifications, eligibility, corrigenda
Makes, standards and capacities; bidder criteria; what each corrigendum changed.
Documents
The backed-up PDF, DGLL's original link, pages, and whether it was read from the text layer or the page images. Unreadable spots are listed.
Filters
Search any word (titles, sites, items), or narrow by directorate, category and year. A card's "All … tenders" button opens its directorate.

Full ledger record

"Full ledger record" on a card opens everything DGLL's ledger contains for that station, section by section, in page order: light, optic, lantern, RACON, NAIS, DGPS, NAVTEX, power, gensets, alternators, solar plants, batteries, chargers, UPS, stabilisers, automation, CCTV, air-conditioners, fire fighting, communication, water supply, land, buildings, staff, approach, postal address, construction cost and commissioning dates.

Summary
Sections, entries, how many are blank / NIL / NA, and how many mention equipment reported broken, removed, defective or not working.
Find
Filters sections by any word.
Values
Verbatim from the PDF's rendered pages. Blank and NIL entries are kept and shown muted. Personal phone numbers are removed.
Downloads
The original PDF, DGLL's page and the record as JSON.

Data & methods

  • Master Ledgers. All 211 PDFs linked from DGLL's regional pages were read twice by Claude, from both the text layer and the page images: once for the light function, once for every section verbatim.
  • Positions. Hand-typed coordinates were checked against OpenStreetMap, Wikidata and the Lighthouse Directory. Digit slips and seconds typed as decimal minutes were corrected only when the fix lands on the same named light; every change is listed in build_notes.txt.
  • Light simulation. One rule for every light, from its own ledger: rhythm, rotation, panels, beam width. Conflicts are shown, not hidden.
  • Equipment years. The earliest installation or commissioning date printed in that equipment's own section. Where a station prints none, the earliest date any ledger records for that network, marked as such.
  • Not active. Murud Janjira's ledger reports the light non-functional for years, so it is off the map. VTS, repeater and decommissioned Loran-C stations are skipped.
  • Parliament. Public access, museums, planned Brahmaputra lights and funding come from Lok Sabha answers of July and August 2026.
  • Tenders. Every document on DGLL's tender pages was downloaded and read by Claude: the text layer where the PDF has one, the page images for scans and garbled Hindi text. Station names were matched to the map by hand-checked spelling variants.
  • Backups. Ledgers, tender documents and station photos are kept as unaltered copies with checksums, so the record survives when a government page changes.
  • Warnings. NHO's India WINS map lists only warnings in force, so the archive is built here: each check stores the response if it changed, a warning keeps the dates it was first and last listed, and is marked ended on the first check that no longer lists it. Positions come from NHO's own geometry (polygon corners in the order the message gives them; arcs and circles drawn from their radius). A warning is linked to a lighthouse only when it names a light or radio aid within 3 km of the station.
  • Pixel animation. Beams, sweeps, NAVTEX waves and RACON rings are drawn in blocks with a few dithered brightness steps, advancing 12 times a second. The timings, reach and bearings stay the ledger's.

Using it for accountability

  • Open a station's full record and look at the summary: blank entries, equipment reported broken or removed, and the dates of the last replacement.
  • Compare the ledger with what you see on a visit: is the light flashing its published characteristic? Is the "public access" lighthouse actually open?
  • Check construction costs, commissioning dates and staffing across stations in the same directorate.
  • Set a station's tenders against its ledger: was the generator, AC or tower repair bought and then still listed broken? Were the same works tendered again and again?
  • Look for amber inconsistencies: a ledger whose rotation speed cannot produce its own rhythm is a ledger nobody has checked.
  • Download light_audit.csv (every light's ledger inputs and derived simulation) or any station's JSON to analyse the whole network. Cite the original PDF when you publish.

Limits

  • The ledgers are DGLL's own documents, of varying dates. A value can be out of date even when transcribed correctly.
  • Transcription can err where a PDF is blurred or garbled; those places are listed as unreadable rather than guessed.
  • Reach is modelled for clear weather and a 5 m eye; real visibility varies.
  • Not for navigation. Always use official charts and Notices to Mariners.
