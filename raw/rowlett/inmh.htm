Lighthouses of India: Maharashtra

The Republic of India (Bhārat in Hindi) is the seventh largest country and second most populous country in the world. It occupies a subcontinent of Asia that projects southward into the North Indian Ocean, separating the Bay of Bengal on the east from the Arabian Sea on the west. Before 1947 it was a patchwork of regions governed as British colonies and semi-independent princely states under British supervision. In 1947 this British Raj was partitioned into the independent states of India and Pakistan. Several French and Portuguese colonies embedded in the territory of India were absorbed in 1950 and 1961, respectively.

India is a federal union of 28 states and 8 union territories. This page lists lighthouses of the western coast of India in the State of Maharashtra, including the greater Mumbai (Bombay) area. This region includes most of the Konkan Coast, the most rugged section of the Indian coastline, where the mountains known as the Sahyādri or Western Ghats rise close behind the shore.

The major lighthouses of India are staffed, and by tradition most of them are open to visitors for a short time during the late afternoon every day; inquire locally concerning the times.

Hindi and English are official languages in India, with English serving as a lingua franca for the entire nation. The Indian Constitution recognizes another 21 languages, called scheduled languages, and each of the coastal states has its own scheduled language in official use. In Marathi, the official language of Maharashtra, the word for a lighthouse is prakāśastambha (प्रकाशस्तंभ) bēṭa is an island, nadī is a river, and bandara is a harbor.

Lighthouses and aids to navigation in India are administered by the Directorate General of Lighthouses and Lightships (DGLL), an agency of the Ministry of Shipping. The coastal lighthouses on this page from the Ratnagiri District northward are maintained by the Mumbai Directorate of DGLL, while those of the Sindhudurg District are maintained by the Goa Directorate. Lighthouses in Mumbai Harbour are maintained by the Mumbai Port Authority.

ARLHS numbers are from the ARLHS World List of Lights. Admiralty numbers are from volume F of the Admiralty List of Lights & Fog Signals. U.S. NGA List numbers are from Publication 112.

Konkan Coast: Lighthouses South of Mumbai

Sindhudurg District (Vengurla Area) Lighthouses
This area is the heart of the very scenic Konkan Coast, backed by the mountains known as the Western Ghats. Many areas of this coast are rarely visited and lack tourist facilities, so current information on these lighthouses is scarce.

*** Vengurla Point (3)
1968 (station established 1869). Active; focal plane 82 m (269 ft); red flash every 5 s.13 m (43 ft) hexagonal cylindrical masonry tower with lantern and gallery, attached to a 1-story keeper's house. Lighthouse painted with black and white horizontal bands; lantern dome painted red. Kedar Sule contributed a 2007 photo , Lisbon Rebello has a closeup photo, Sushant Dabholkar has a 2023 closeup, Rinat Khairetdinov has a 2019 closeup, and Google has a satellite view. Located atop a seaside bluff at Vengurla. Accessible by road. Site open, tower open daily 4 to 5:30 pm. . ARLHS IND-120; Admiralty F0600; NGA 27800.
Vengurla Rocks (Burnt Islands) (1)
1870. Inactive since 1890. Approx. 10 m (33 ft) octagonal stone tower, lantern removed long ago, centered on a square building. Open fires were displayed from the lighthouse until the lantern and optical equipment arrived from England in 1876. The lighthouse was described as having a focal plane height of 40 m (131 ft). Rajesh Prabhu has a 2022 photo, a 15-second video shows the lighthouse, a very distant view from the sea (third photo on the page) is available, and Bing has an indistinct satellite view. The Vengurla Rocks are bare rocks best known as a breeding site for a rare bird, the Indian swiftlet. Located on the westernmost reef of the Vengurla Rocks about 800 m (1/2 mi) west of the 1890 lighthouse. Accessible only by boat and landing appears difficult or impossible. Site and tower closed.
Vengurla Rocks (Burnt Islands) (2)
1890 (British; station established 1870). Active; focal plane 49 m (161 ft); two white flashes every 20 s. 21 m (69 ft) round cast iron tower with lantern and gallery, painted with red and white horizontal bands. A 2021 photo is at right, Lighthouse Digest has a photo, Rajesh Prabhu has a 2022 photo, Kaina Shaikh has a 2022 aerial photo, Bhushan Parab has a view from the sea, an Instagram photo is available, and Google has a satellite view. Located in the Arabian Sea about 11 km (7 mi) off the coast and 20 km (13 mi) west northwest of Vengurla. Accessible only by boat and landing appears difficult or impossible. Site and tower closed. ARLHS IND-009; Admiralty F0598; NGA 27804.

Malvan (Malwan) Bay Range Rear
Date unknown (station established 1903). Active; focal plane 18 m (59 ft); green flash every 3 s. 14 m (46 ft) square steel skeletal tower. Upper portion of the tower painted red, lower part white. No photo available but Bing has an indistinct satellite view. The front light is on a short mast. This light guides local fishermen and is displayed only during fair weather when they are at sea. Lights were displayed at Malwan at least as early as 1878, and a light on a mast was established in 1903. Located behind the beach at Malvan, a village about 32 km (20 mi) north of Vengurla. Site status unknown. Admiralty F0596.1; NGA 27824.
Achara (Achra) Point (2)
2014. Active; focal plane 49 m (161 ft); two white flashes every 6 s. 17 m (56 ft) round concrete tower with lantern and gallery. Lighthouse painted white, lantern roof red. Abhay Aparaj has a 2019 photo and a closeup of this new lighthouse, Nikhil Karangutkar has a 2022 photo, Jayanti Dhuri has a very distant view and Google has a satellite view. NGA lists a 9 m (30 ft) skeletal tower at this location, and an Indian Notice to Mariners identified that light as "temporarily extinguished" in 2012. Located on a steep headland across the estuary entrance at Achra, a village about 50 km (30 mi) north of Vengurla. Site status unknown. Admiralty F0592; NGA 27832.

Ragapur Bay (Wagapur, Wajapur, Tarapur) Point (2)
1958 (station established 1873). Active; focal plane 46 m (151 ft); white flash every 15 s. 23 m (75 ft) square cylindrical masonry tower with lantern and gallery, attached to a 1-story concrete keeper's cottage. Lighthouse painted with black and white horizontal bands; lantern dome painted red. Sai Kumar Kodi has the DGLL photo, Prathamesh Dhawde has a closeup photo, Wikimapia has several photos, and Google has a satellite view. The original lighthouse was a 6.5 m (21 ft) tower. ARLHS IND-122; Admiralty F0586; NGA 27840.

Raigad District Lighthouses
Nanwell (Nanvell) Point (Rajpuri Point)
1964 (station established 1884). Active; focal plane 66 m (217 ft); three white flashes every 15 s. 19 m (62 ft) octagonal masonry tower with lantern and gallery painted with red and white horizontal bands. Prajyot Sale has the 2022 photo at right, Pritam Patil has a 2018 closeup photo, Sachchida Nand Arya has a photo, Wikimapia has photos, and Google has a satellite view. The original light was a 10.5 m (34 ft) white tower. In 1998 Nilesh Korgaonkar had a long hike from Nanvell to reach the lighthouse, where he had tea with the keepers. Located on a promontory at the southern entrance to the Rajpuri Creek estuary, about 80 km (50 mi) west of Mangaon. Site open but quite difficult to reach, tower may be open by arrangement with the keepers. ARLHS IND-104; Admiralty F0562; NGA 27908.

* Korlai Fort (Chaul)
1955. Active; focal plane 33 m (108 ft); white flash every 3 s. 16 m (52 ft) square cylindrical masonry tower with lantern and gallery, attached to the front of a 2-story masonry keeper's house. Tower painted with black and white horizontal bands; lantern dome painted red. Ketan Kadam's 2024 photo is below right, Harshal Sonawane has a 2019 photo, Mithila Rane has a photo, Vinit Maheshwari has a 2007 photo, Siddhesh Balsaraf has a street view, and Google has a satellite view and a fuzzy street view. Ruins of the early 16th century Korlai fort, built in a successful campaign to expel the Portuguese from Chaul, are on the hill above the lighthouse; a long stairway connects the fort and the light station. Located on a promontory on the north side of the entrance to Chaul, about 18 km (11 mi) south of Alibag and 40 km (25 mi) south of the Mumbai (Bombay) metropolitan area. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-081; Admiralty F0558; NGA 27928.
Chaul Kadu Reefs
1870 (British). Active; focal plane 17 m (56 ft); red flash every 3 s. 19 m (62 ft) round cylindrical masonry tower with gallery, painted white. Lantern removed. In front of the lighthouse is a prow-shaped masonry wall meant to break the force of any waves that might reach the tower. The lighthouse was built with a refuge room to shelter shipwrecked sailors. Bing has a satellite view. Located about 5 km (3 mi) offshore and about 10 km (6 mi) southwest of Alibag. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. ARLHS IND-001; Admiralty F0557.6; NGA 27924.
* Alibag (Kolaba Fort, Kulaba Fort)
Date unknown (station established 1890). Active; focal plane 10 m (33 ft); continuous light, white or green depending on direction. Approx. 7 m (23 ft) post rising from a square stone tower mounted on the southernmost bastion of the Kolaba Fort. Siddharth Panigrahi has a photo, Rohit Hannurkar has a 2017 street view, a closeup is available, and Google has a satellite view of the bastion. The Kolaba Fort is a famous 17th century Maratha stronghold, a center of resistance to British occupation. Located on an island about 2 km (1.25 mi) southwest of Alibag. A popular tourist destination, the fort is accessible by passenger ferry from Alibag. Site open, tower closed. Admiralty F0557; NGA 27932.
** Kanhoji (Khanoji) Angre (Khanderi Island, Kennery Island)
1867 (British). Active; focal plane 47 m (154 ft); two flashes every 10 s, white or red depending on direction. 23 m (75 ft) octagonal cut stone tower with lantern and gallery, attached to a 2-story keeper's house. 1st order Fresnel lens in use. The tower is unpainted; lantern painted white; lantern roof is gray metallic. An enclosed observation room has been built on the gallery. Goraksha Ghawate's 2018 photo is at the top of this page, Wikimedia has Anand Ranade's 2015 photo, Pankaj Bhalbar has a 2024 photo, Huelse has a historic postcard view, Forand has a second postcard view, and Google has a satellite view. Khanderi Island is also the site of a 17th century fort. The island is managed by the Mumbai Port Trust. The lighthouse has been renamed for Khanhoji Angre, an early 18th century Maratha admiral who used the island as a base to attack British and Portuguese ships along the west coast of India. In April 2013 it was announced that the station would be opened to visitors for the first time; a new jetty was built to support regular tours from Mumbai. Located about 6 km (4.5 mi) offshore and 10 km (6 mi) northwest of Alibag. Site open, tower open 10 am to 5 pm daily with tours from Mumbai available; there is a very distant view from the mainland. Operator/site manager: Mumbai Port Trust. ARLHS IND-093; Admiralty F0556; NGA 27936

Mumbai Area Lighthouses

Mumbai (known officially as Bombay until 1995) is India's greatest port and second most populous city. It is also the capital of Maharashtra state. The historic and central portion of the city is located on Salsette Island, separated from the mainland by the city's harbor and estuaries called Thane Creek and Vasai Creek. The two creeks are the main distributaries of the Ulhas River. 15 million people live on Salsette Island and the metropolitan population of Mumbai is at least 23 million.

The east (mainland) side of Mumbai Harbor, including Butcher and Elephanta Islands, is in the city of Navi Mumbai (New Mumbai), developed since in 1970s. The city is part of the Raigad District and has a population of 1.6 million.

Trabas has Cameron's 2010 photo, another 2010 closeup also shows that the lantern had been removed, and Google has a satellite view. Sourendu Gupta's 2006 photo shows the lighthouse with its lantern intact. Swimming races are held between the lighthouse and the Gateway of India, a famous landmark on the Mumbai waterfront. In March 2017 the Port Trust announced plans to restore and develop the lighthouse as a tourist attraction, including decks around the tower to support a restaurant. At last report these plans were under review and negotiation with the Environment Ministry and the Mumbai Heritage Conservation Committee. Obviously nothing has been done and unless something is done soon there will be no lighthiuse to save. Located about 2.5 km southeast of the Gateway to India. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. Operator/site manager: Mumbai Port Trust. ARLHS IND-008; Admiralty F0516; NGA 27956.

Mumbai Ocean Side Lighthouses
Prongs Reef (Colaba Point)
1874 (British; lightship station established 1842). Active; focal plane 41 m (135 ft); white flash every 10 s. 44 m (144 ft) round tapered stone tower with lantern and two galleries, mounted on a stone pier. 1st order Fresnel lens in use. The tower is painted white with a red horizontal band, and the supporting pier is painted black. A 2023 photo is at right, Milan Vyas has a 2021 photo, Mayur Mangela has a photo, Sahil Chauhan has a 2022 photo, Trabas has Cameron's 2010 view, a view from shore is available, Huelse has a historic postcard image, and Google has a good satellite view. This historic offshore lighthouse, marking the entrance to Mumbai Harbour, replaced the onshore Colaba Point lighthouse (see below under Lost Lighthouses). It is one of the few offshore lighthouses in the world still staffed; keepers alternate in 15-day shifts. In its early days, the lighthouse was equipped with a cannon to repel pirates. Located about 2.5 km (1.5 mi) south of Colaba Point, the southernmost point of Salsette Island. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. Operator/site manager: DGLL. ARLHS IND-007; Admiralty F0514; NGA 27952.

Bassein (Vasai Creek) (2)
2019. Active; focal plane 5 m (17 ft); quick-flashing red light. Approx. 7 m (23 ft) round concrete or stone tower. * Arnala (5)
2019 (station established 1860). Active; focal plane 33 m (108 ft); three flashes every 15 s, white or red depending on direction. 30 m (98 ft) round concrete tower with lantern and double gallery, painted with black and white spiral bands. Information available on lost lighthouses:

  • Colaba Point (1847?-early 1900s), Mumbai. Forand has an 1875 photo of this 27 m (89 ft) lighthouse, which was located at the southern tip of Salsette Island in Mumbai. Various early light lists and Trethewey's history give 1847 as the date of this station, but other historical sources say that there was a lighthouse on the point in the late 1700s. The light was apparently deactivated when the Prongs Reef Light was completed in 1874, but it remained in use as a signal station. The last known reference to it is in 1906, but we do not know when it was demolished. There is no trace of the station remaining today.
