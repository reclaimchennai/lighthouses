Lighthouses of India: Western Gujarat

The Republic of India (Bhārat in Hindi) is the seventh largest country and second most populous country in the world. It occupies a subcontinent of Asia that projects southward into the North Indian Ocean, separating the Bay of Bengal on the east from the Arabian Sea on the west. Before 1947 it was a patchwork of regions governed as British colonies and semi-independent princely states under British supervision. In 1947 this British Raj was partitioned into the independent states of India and Pakistan. Several French and Portuguese colonies embedded in the territory of India were absorbed in 1950 and 1961, respectively.

India is a federal union of 28 states and 8 union territories. The westernmost state, Gujarat, has the longest coastline of any of the states and the largest number of lighthouses. The broad Kathiawar Peninsula, in the center of the state's coastline, separates two major inlets of the Arabian Sea, the Gulf of Khambhat on the southeast and the Gulf of Kutch on the northwest. This page lists lighthouses of the western part of Gujarat surrounding the Gulf of Kutch, including the Devbhumi Dwarka and Jamnagar districts at the western end of the Kathiawar peninsula as well as the large Kachchh (Kutch) district on the north side of the Gulf of Kutch.

The political history of this region is quite complex. Under the British Raj the majority of the modern territory of Gujarat was divided into princely states that had control over local economic affairs. Several of these Indian states built lighthouses, including several that survive. After India became independent in 1947 Kutch was established as a state and the Kathiawar peninsula was included in the state of Saurashtra. In 1956 Kutch and Saurashtra were merged into the large state of Bombay, and then in 1960 Bombay state was divided, with its northern portion split off as the state of Gujarat.

Gujarat is divided into 33 districts and the districts are divided into county-level units called talukas.

Hindi and English are official languages in India, with English serving as a lingua franca for the entire nation. The Indian Constitution recognizes another 21 languages, called scheduled languages, and each of the coastal states has its own scheduled language in official use. In Gujarati, the official language of Gujarat, word for a lighthouse is dīvādāṇḍī (દીવાદાંડી); tāpu is an island, nadī is a river, and bandara is a harbor.

Lighthouses and aids to navigation in India are administered by the Directorate General of Lighthouses and Lightships (DGLL), an agency of the Ministry of Shipping. Lighthouses on the south coast of the Kathiawar Peninsula are maintained by the Jamnagar Directorate and those of the Gulf of Kutch are maintained by the Gandhidham Directorate.

ARLHS numbers are from the ARLHS World List of Lights. Admiralty numbers are from volume F of the Admiralty List of Lights & Fog Signals. U.S. NGA List numbers are from Publication 112.

Western Kathiawar Peninsula Lighthouses

Devbhumi Dwarka District: Dwarka Taluka Lighthouses
The Devbhumi Dwarka District includes the westernmost tip of the Kathiawar Peninsula and the southern entrance to the Gulf of Kutch. Split from the Jamnagar District in 2013, the district has a population of about 750,000.

*** Dwarka Point (2)
1962 (station established 1866). Active; focal plane 48 m (157 ft); two white flashes every 20 s. 43 m (141 ft) square masonry tower with lantern and gallery, attached to a 1-story keeper's house/harbor control center. Lighthouse painted with horizontal black and white bands. A 2024 photo is at the top of this page, a 2018 photo is available, Elvin Lonan has a 2010 photo, Tanmay Vora has a 2008 photo, Wikimedia has a distant view, Harshang Chhaya has a closeup street view, and Google has a satellite view. This is the westernmost lighthouse of Saurashtra and a well-known tourist attraction. The original lighthouse, an 18 m (59 ft) square masonry tower, was built by the native state of Baroda. Located at the end of the peninsula sheltering the west side of the harbor of Dwarka. Site open, tower open daily. . ARLHS IND-133; Admiralty F0396; NGA 28300.
* Kachhigadh (2)
1983 (station established 1977). Active; focal plane 34 m (112 ft); four white flashes every 15 s. 30 m (98 ft) round cylindrical concrete tower with lantern and gallery, painted with horizontal red and white bands. Arvind Prajapati has the 2022 photo seen at right, Vatsal Mandavia has a closeup 2021 photo, Sejul Antala has a closeup, R. Ramesh has a street view from the nearby beach, and Google has a satellite view. The station was established with a temporary light on a "cabin" in 1977. It marks the southern approach to the Gulf of Kachchh (Kutch). Located about 15 km (9 mi) north of Dwarka. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-142; Admiralty F0397; NGA 28304.
* Mithapur
Date unknown. Active; focal plane 53 m (174 ft); continuous red light. 49.5 m (163 ft) building. A giant Tata Chemicals plant dominates Mithapur and its main office building is almost certainly the location of the light. A photo is available and Google has a street view and satellite view. Mithapur is about 10 km (6 mi) southwest of Okha. Site open, tower closed. Admiralty F0393; NGA 28320.
* Okha (Vomani Point, Humani Point)
1956. Active; focal plane 26 m (85 ft); three white flashes every 25 s. 23 m (75 ft) square cylindrical concrete tower with lantern and gallery, painted with horizontal red and white bands. Niraj Kumar has a photo. Google's satellite view shows the 60 m (197 ft) round VTS vessel tracking tower built next to the historic lighthouse in 2012. Wikimedia has a view taken from the VTS tower. Okha lies at the end of a north-pointing peninsula that defines the southern entrance to the Gulf of Kachchh (Kutch). After independence western India lost the use of the port of Karachi, Pakistan, so it became important to develop port facilities in the Gulf. This lighthouse was the first major improvement to navigational aids in this development. Located on Vomani (Humani) Point near the tip of the peninsula, adjacent to a historic fort. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-014; Admiralty F0388; NGA 28312.

Samiyani Island (Okha Port)
1876. Active; focal plane 22 m (72 ft); flash every 5 s, white or red depending on direction. 20 m (66 ft) round sandstone tower with lantern and gallery, mounted on a square stone base. Lighthouse painted white; lantern dome is red. A 2018 photo is at right, Kashyap Atri has a 2019 photo, Vishal Ahir has a 2024 view from the sea, and Google has a satellite view. Built by the princely state of Baroda, this elegant and historic lighthouse marks the entrance to Okha harbor. It stands on an island 1.5 km (1 mi) north of Okha Point, the tip of the peninsula. Boat transportation to the island can be hired in Okha. Located in the center of the island. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-018; Admiralty F0390; NGA 28308.
Samiyani Island North (4)
2002 (station established 1935). Active; focal plane 11 m (36 ft); green flash every 3 s. 9 m (30 ft) round cylindrical concrete tower, mounted on a concrete foundation. Lighthouse painted with black and white horizontal bands. Chetan Kanzariya has a distant view and Google has an indistinct satellite view. The original light on a skeletal tower was replaced by a concrete tower in 1957. That lighthouse was destroyed by a tropical cyclone on 9 June 1998. It was replaced by a temporary skeletal tower, and then by the present lighthouse, which probably resembles the 1957 tower. Located in the water off the northeast tip of Samiyani Island. , marking the end of a long shoal. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. ARLHS IND-163; Admiralty F0389; NGA 28316.
Bural Reef (Chanka Reef)
1979. Active; focal plane 15 m (49 ft); quick-flashing white light. 12 m (39 ft) round cylindrical concrete tower with three galleries but no lantern, painted white. Lightphotos.net has an older photo, Indian Logistics Info.has a page with a photo, and Bing has a satellite view. The dangerous Bural Reef extends east northeast from Okha Point. Located about 25 km (15 mi) east northeast of Okha. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. ARLHS IND-126; Admiralty F0386.5; NGA 28328.
Chank Tapu (Chanka Island) (2)
1988 (station established 1934). Active; focal plane 14 m (46 ft); white flash every 15 s. 8 m (26 ft) square cylindrical skeletal tower with gallery, mounted on a large square concrete base. Wikimapia has an older photo and Bing has a satellite view. Lighthouse painted white; the small lantern is red. A stone daybeacon was placed on the reef in 1885; a skeletal tower was added in 1928 but apparently it was not lighted until 1934. Located at the northeastern end of the extensive Chanka Reef system, about 35 km (2 mi) east northeast of Okha. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. Admiralty F0386; NGA 28332.

Devbhumi Dwarka District: Khambhalia Taluka Lighthouses
Kalubhar (Karumbhar) Island (Salaya)
1884. Active; focal plane 27 m (89 ft); three white flashes every 15 s. 20 m (66 ft) round stone tower with lantern and gallery, attached to a 1-story keeper's house. Lighthouse painted white; lantern dome is red. Prashant Chauhan's 2020 photo is at right, Mukeshkumar Mali has a distant photo, and Google has a satellite view. This lighthouse, which marks the entrance to the harbor of Salaya, was built by the princely state of Nawanagar. The tower was restored and increased in height in 1961. Located just off the west end of the island about 20 km (12.5 mi) north of Salaya. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. ARLHS IND-169; Admiralty F0384; NGA 28336.
Pathfinder Inlet (Vadinar) Range Front (2 towers)
2015 (station established 2008). Active; focal plane unknown; continuous red light. Mast atop a tall square skeletal tower, height unknown. No photo available but Google has a satellite view. The new port of Vadinar serves area oil refineries. The satellite view shows a second tower just to the north; it's not clear if this is the inactive original tower or if it is an active auxiliary light. Located a short distance south of the port area. Accessible only by boat. Site open, tower closed. Operator: Deendayal Port Trust. Admiralty F0383.75.
Pathfinder Inlet (Vadinar) Range Rear (2 towers)
2015 (station established 2008). Active; focal plane unknown; flashing white light. Mast atop a tall square skeletal tower, height unknown. No photo available but Google has a satellite view. The satellite view shows a second tower just to the north; it's not clear if this is the inactive original tower or if it is an active auxiliary light. Located 750 m (1/2 mi) south of the front light. Accessible only by boat. Site open, tower closed. Operator: Deendayal Port Trust. Admiralty F0383.751.

Jamnagar District: Jamnagar Taluka Lighthouses
Sikka Range Front (2?)
2011. Active; focal plane unknown; continuous red light. Skeletal tower atop a square platform supported by piles. No photo available but Google has a distant satellite view. Sikka is a traditional fishing port that now also hosts several industrial plants. The range lights were moved about 500 m (0.3 mi) southeast in 2021. Located a short distance east of the pier at Sikka. Accessible only by boat. Site open, tower closed. Operator: Sikka Ports and Terminals. Admiralty F0382.51; NGA 28347.
Sikka Range Rear (2?)
2011. Active; focal plane unknown; continuous red light. Skeletal tower atop a square platform supported by piles. No photo available but Google has a distant satellite view. Located 500 m (1/3 mi) southeast of the front light. Accessible only by boat. Site open, tower closed. Operator: Sikka Ports and Terminals. Admiralty F0382.511; NGA 28347.5.
Pirotan (Pirothan) Island (2)
1958 (station established 1898). Active; focal plane 26 m (85 ft); white flash every 20 s. 21 m (69 ft) round masonry tower with lantern and gallery, painted with horizontal black and white bands. Jayesh Kumar's 2020 photo is at right, Wikimedia has a sunrise photo by S. Ballal, Kumar has another 2020 photo, and Google has a satellite view. A seawall protects this highly exposed lighthouse. Pirotan is the beginning of the approach to Bedi, the seaport of the city of Jamnagar. Located on a small island 10 km (6 mi) northwest of Rozi. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. ARLHS IND-157; Admiralty F0380; NGA 28352.
Mawadi Point (Pirotan Northeast) (2)
1958 (station established 1928). Active; focal plane 8 m (26 ft); red flash every 10 s. 8 m (26 ft) short round tower mounted atop a round cylindrical stone tower. The upper tower is painted red with a white horizontal band. The lower tower is unpainted brown stone. Tushar Chhatrara has a photo but the light is not shown in Google's satellite view. The light marks the end of a reef extending northeast from Pirotan Island. The first light was a skeletal tower built on a daybecaon established in 1867. Located about 2.25 km (1.4 mi) northeast of the modern lighthouse. Site open, and nothing prevents climbing on the lighthouse. Admiralty F0378; NGA 28356.
* Rozi Port (Bedi Bandar, Bedi Bundar, Bedi) (2)
Date unknown. Inactive since 2002. Approx. 25 m (82 ft) round cylindrical vessel traffic control tower painted with red and white horizontal bands. Hari Prasad Kanithi has a 2018 photo, a 2016 photo (first photo on the page) is available, Tanmay Vora has a 2012 view from across the harbor, and Google has a satellite view. This tower replaced a 12 m (39 ft) square skeletal tower with a small equipment closet in the base. The light was deactivated because it became redundant on the brightly lit Rozi Pier. Located on the quay at Bedi Bandar, the seaport serving the city of Jamnagar. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-124; ex-Admiralty F0376; ex-NGA 28358.
* Rozi (Rojhi)
1867. Inactive since 1976. Approx. 13 m (43 ft) round cylindrical stone tower, a bastion at the northwest corner of the fortified Rozi Mata temple, now in ruins. Sandeepsinh Sodha has a photo and Google has a satellite view. The original light was on a mast atop the tower; in 1897 it was replaced by a "cast iron pedestal" with a 6th order Fresnel lens. The light was moved to the new Rozi Pier in 1976. Located just east of the base of the pier. Site open, tower status unknown.

Rozi Kalvan (Rozi Kalyan) (3?)
2024 (?) (station established 1927). The 1976 lighthouse was a 9 m (30 ft) round masonry tower on a concrete base, painted with horizontal black and white bands. This light on the approach to Bedi was damaged in the great earthquake of 26 January 2001 and collapsed "subsequently," according to Indian Logistics. The light was dropped from the Admiralty list in 2008 and it disappeared from Google satellite views. It was replaced by a buoy. However the current Google satellite view shows a new tower on a hexagonal platform. This light is not charted by C-Map and not listed by the Admiralty as of November 2025. Located east of Rozi Island and about 6 km (3.5 mi) north of the Bedi waterfront. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. ARLHS IND-161; ex-Admiralty F0374; ex-NGA 28360.

Jamnagar District: Jodiya Taluka Lighthouses
Mungra Reef
1890 (rebuilt and lit in 1997; rebuilt a second time in 1999). Active; focal plane 10 m (33 ft); two white flashes every 10 s. 10 m (33 ft) round cylindrical concrete tower on a circular masonry pier. Tower painted red with one white horizontal band. The Indian Logistics Info photo is at right, but the light is not seen in Bing's satellite view. The lighthouse marks a dangerous reef on the approach to Jodiya Bandar. The native state of Nawanagar built this beacon. It was rebuilt in 1999 after being heavily damaged by a tropical cyclone on 9 June 1998. Located in the Gulf of Kachchh about 12 km (7.5 mi) northwest of Jodiya Bandar. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. ARLHS IND-151; Admiralty F0367.8; NGA 28366.
* Jodiya (Jodia) Bandar (3)
2002 (station established 1903). Active; focal plane 31 m (102 ft); white flash every 10 s. 30 m (98 ft) skeletal tower, painted white. A 2021 photo and a distant 2025 view are available and Google has a satellite view. The 1993 lighthouse, a round cylindrical masonry tower with gallery attached to a small square 1-story equipment room, was destroyed by the earthquake of 26 January 2001. The original lighthouse was a small cast iron tower purchased from Chance Brothers by the native state of Nawanagar. Located at Jodiya (Jodia), a small port on the upper Gulf of Kachchh. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-141; Admiralty F0368; NGA 28368.

Kachchh (Kutch) District Lighthouses

Kutch (officially spelled Kachchh) includes all the territory between the Gulf of Kutch and the border of Pakistan to the north. Kutch was a state from 1950 to 1956. It entered Gujarat state as a single district, making it by far the largest district not only in Gujarat but in all of India.

Gandhidham Taluka Lighthouses
Navlakhi (Navlakhi Bet, Kandla Port) (3)
2012 (station established 1932). Active; focal plane 30 m (198 ft); three white flashes every 10 s. 30 m (98 ft) round concrete tower with lantern, gallery, and an observation level, very similar to the Bhadreswar lighthouse at right. Tower painted with horizontal red and white bands. Saiad Sekh has a 2023 photo, Rajesh Shah has a distant view, and Google has a satellite view. The light guides ships approaching the port facility at Navlakhi at the head of the Gulf of Kachchh. The original light was replaced in 1985 by a 24 m (79 ft) skeletal tower. That lighthouse was knocked out of service by the earthquake of 26 January 2001, but it was restored and returned to service the following month. Located on the northwestern tip of Navlakhi Island. Accessible only by boat. Site and tower closed. ARLHS IND-155; Admiralty F0370; NGA 28376.
Tekra (Tuna) (3?)
Date unknown (station established 1892). Active; focal plane 17 m (56 ft); white flash every 7 s. Formerly described as a 14 m (46) "metal column" atop a masonry tower, painted with black and white horizontal bands. The light is now described by NGA as a "metal column" with black and white horizontal bands. No photo available and Google now has a satellite view showing what appears to be a round concrete tower. The light guides ships entering Kandla Port, a greatly expanded port facility at the head of the Gulf of Kachchh. The original light was described as an "iron column." Located on the west side of a creek entrance in the Kandla Port complex south of Anjar, near the head of the Gulf of Kachchh. Site status unknown. Operator: Adanai Ports Tuna Terminal. Admiralty F0366; NGA 28380.

Mundra Taluka Lighthouses
* Bhadreswar (Nakti Creek)

A 2023 photo is at right and Google has a satellite view. This light is mounted on a vessel traffic control tower. Located at Bhadreswar, a village about 30 km (19 mi) northwest of Mundra. #Mundra New Port (Mitha Port) (3)
1985 (station established 1951). Inactive since 2011 and probably demolished. This was a 7 m (23 ft) square cylindrical concrete column attached to a 1-story concrete equipment room. No photo available and Google's satellite view does not show the light. A new port, sheltered from the summer monsoon winds, was developed at Mundra during the 1950s. In 1998-2001 a major new Mundra Port was developed by private interests. The original lighthouse was a skeletal tower. Located on the Mitha quay, a small boat quay within the Mundra port area. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-150; ex-Admiralty F0364; ex-NGA 28400.

Mandvi Taluka Lighthouses
#Rawal Pir (3)
1969 (station established 1936). Inactive since 2020 and apparently demolished. This was a 7 m (23 ft) square cylindrical concrete column attached to a 1-story concrete equipment room. Lighthouse painted white. Tarak Shanishchara's 2024 photo, taken from the top of the current lighthouse, does not show the older light. The original light was mounted on the tomb of a revered Sufi master (known as a pir). A separate tower was provided in 1947 and replaced in 1969. Located close to the Ravdpir (Rawal Pir) Temple complex about 3 km (2 mi) east of Mandvi. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-160.
* Rawal Pir (4)
2020 (station established 1936). Active; focal plane 40 m (131 ft); green flash every 3 s. 31 m (102 ft) round concrete tower with lantern and two galleries painted with red and white spiral bands. Vinay Ahirwar has a 2025 photo, a 2022 closeup photo is available, Samat Vagha has a 2021 photo, and Google has a satellite view. The listed location is the same as for the original light. Admiralty F0360; NGA 28404.
* Mandvi Breakwater (3)
1957 (station established 1890). Active; focal plane 8 m (26 ft); red flash every 3 s. 7 m (23 ft) square cylindrical concrete tower, upper half painted red and lower half white. Sahil Rayma has a 2017 photo and a 2020 photo, Parth Parmar has a photo showing the lighthouse in silhouette, Chandresh Malamwala has a distant view, Aresh Hirani has a street view across the harbor entrance, and Google has a satellite view. Located at the end of the west breakwater at Mandvi Harbour. Accessible by walking the pier. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-146; Admiralty F0358; NGA 28408.
* #Mandvi (Mandvi Fort) (3)
1964 (station established 1856). Demolished in 2001. This was a round cylindrical tower with lantern and gallery mounted on a round stone tower 17th century fortress of Mandvi; the light was listed with a focal plane of 35 m (115 ft). Google has a satellite view of the fortress. Heavily damaged in the earthquake of 26 January 2001, the lighthouse was pulled down the following month. Located on the south side of the new lighthouse. Site open, status of the fortress tower unknown.
* Mandvi (5)
2012 (station established 1856). Active; focal plane 63 m (207 ft); three white flashes, separated by 5 s, every 30 s. 30 m (98 ft) round cylindrical concrete vessel traffic control tower with two circular observation levels. Lighthouse painted with red and white horizontal bands. Hitendra Vankani's 2024 photo is at right, Tanmay Vora has a closeup photo, Hardik Dhayani has a 2015 photo, Vinay Toprani has a photo, and Google has a 2023 street view and a satellite view. The 1964 lighthouse (previous entry) was a cylindrical concrete tower with lantern and gallery; that lighthouse was demolished after being heavily damaged by the earthquake of 26 January 2001. It was replaced by a skeletal tower until the present tower was built. Mandvi has been the major port of the Kachchh district for many centuries. The lighthouse is built on the historic Mandvi Fort, from which lights were often displayed when friendly ships were expected. Although DGLL dates the modern light station to 1873, Ken Trethewey's research indicated that the British built a lighthouse on the fort in 1856. Light lists indicate that a taller tower was built in 1889. Located near the waterfront in Mandvi. Site open, tower probably open but we have no information on this. ARLHS IND-145; Admiralty F0357; NGA 28411.

Abdasa Taluka Lighthouses
* Chhachhi (Kachchigadh) (2)
1964 (station established 1957). Active; focal plane 33 m (108 ft); three white flashes every 15 s. 31 m (102 ft) square cylindrical masonry tower with lantern and gallery. Lighthouse painted with horizontal black and white bands; lantern roof painted red. DGLL's photo shows the lighthouse and the much taller traffic control tower that has stood next to it since 2008. Saurabh Parmar has a 2019 photo of the two towers, Niraj Kumar also has a photo, and Google has a satellite view of the two towers. This is an important lighthouse marking the northern entrance to the Gulf of Kachchh. Located near the village of Chhachhi about 50 km (30 mi) north northwest of Mandvi. Site open, although roads in the area are poor; tower closed. ARLHS IND-015; Admiralty F0356; NGA 28416.
* Jakhau (2)
1965 (station established 1957). Active; focal plane 46 m (151 ft); white flash every 10 s. 41 m (135 ft) round cylindrical concrete tower with lantern and gallery, painted with horizontal red and white bands. Prashant Chauhan's 2024 photo is at right, Chandrakant Swain has a 2018 photo, DGLL has a photo of the 60 m (197 ft) traffic control tower that stands next to the lighthouse, Chandrakant Swain has a photo of both towers, and Google has a satellite view. The original lighthouse here was replaced in 1964. Located on the main quay of Jakhau, a fishing port in western Kachchh. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-138; Admiralty F0355; NGA 28420.

Lakhpat Taluka Lighthouse
* Koteshwar
2014 (?). Active; focal plane 60 m (197 ft); two white flashes every 15 s. Approx. 57 m (187 ft) round cylindrical concrete tower with lantern and gallery, painted with black and white horizontal bands. Jay Thakker has a 2023 photo, Tanmay Vora has a photo, Lightphotos.net has a photo, and Google has a satellite view. This is both the northernmost and the westernmost lighthouse of India. It has strategic value, standing to the east of the disputed Rann of Kutch area on the Pakistani frontier. Located on the south side of the village of Koteshwar facing the inlet known as Kori Creek. Site open, tower closed. Admiralty F0354; NGA 28420.5.
