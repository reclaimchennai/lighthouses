Lighthouses of India: Tamil Nadu and Puducherry

The Republic of India (Bhārat in Hindi) is the seventh largest country and second most populous country in the world. It occupies a subcontinent of Asia that projects southward into the North Indian Ocean, separating the Bay of Bengal on the east from the Arabian Sea on the west. Before 1947 it was a patchwork of regions governed as British colonies and semi-independent princely states under British supervision. In 1947 this British Raj was partitioned into the independent states of India and Pakistan. Several French and Portuguese colonies embedded in the territory of India were absorbed in 1950 and 1961, respectively.

India is a federal union comprising 28 states and 8 union territories. This page lists lighthouses of Tamil Nadu, the state on the east side of the southern tip of the Indian subcontinent. Embedded in Tamil Nadu are the Puducherry and Karaikal districts of the Union Territory of Puducherry (formerly called Pondicherry), the former French India.

The Tamil Nadu coastline, facing the Bay of Bengal, is almost everywhere low and sandy with numerous offshore shoals. Chennai (Madras) is the state capital and largest port but there are many smaller ports. The disastrous tsunami of 26 December 2004 caused great destruction and loss of life on this coast but no lighthouses were destroyed.

India's lighthouse heritage is British. However, only a fraction of the original British lighthouses remain in use; new towers have been built at most of the stations. India has also built many lighthouses at new locations in recent years. Nearly all of the larger Indian lighthouses are staffed and by long tradition the staff will receive visitors at a specific time, usually late in the afternoon. Several lighthouses have been developed as tourist attractions and have longer hours.

Hindi and English are official languages in India, with English serving as a lingua franca for the entire nation. The Indian Constitution recognizes another 21 languages, called scheduled languages, and each of the coastal states has its own scheduled language in official use. In Tamil, the official language of Tamil Nadu, the word for a lighthouse is kalaṅkarai (கலங்கரை); tīvu is an island, nati is a river, and tuṟaimukam is a harbor.

Lighthouses and aids to navigation in India are administered by the Directorate General of Lighthouses and Lightships (DGLL), an agency of the Ministry of Shipping. The lighthouses on this page are maintained by the Chennai Directorate of DGLL.

The Tamil word for a lighthouse is kalaṅkarai (கலங்கரை); tīvu is an island, nati is a river, and tuṟaimukam is a harbor.

ARLHS numbers are from the ARLHS World List of Lights. Admiralty numbers are from volume F of the Admiralty List of Lights & Fog Signals. U.S. NGA List numbers are from Publication 112.

*** Pulicat (Pazhaverkadu) (2)
1986 (station established 1862). Active; focal plane 53 m (174 ft); white flash every 5 s. 49 m (160 ft) hexagonal cylindrical concrete tower with lantern and gallery, painted with horizontal black and white bands. * Madras (2)
1844 (station established 1796). Inactive since 1894. 49 m (160 ft) round fluted stone tower with lantern and gallery. Lantern dome painted red. Selvakumar Chandrasekaran's 2022 photo is at right, a 2018 photo is available, Balu Velachery has a 2005 photo, Karthik Raghu has a closeup, Huelse has a historic postcard view of this tower and the High Court building (next entry), Forand has a historic postcard view showing the tower with its original lantern, and Google has a satellite view. Located in downtown Madras, now called Chennai. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-027.

Kanchipuram District Lighthouses
*** Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram) (1) (temple)
1887. Inactive since 1900. Approx. 20 m (66 ft) square stone structure with gallery atop a monolithic stone temple (carved from a single rock), dating from around the start of the 8th century AD. Wikimedia has a closeup, a 2022 view is from the top of the lighthouse, a distant photo is available, and Forand has a historic postcard view. Fires were lit atop this temple for centuries as a guide to seamen, and in 1887 the British placed a lantern atop the temple for the same purpose. The lantern was removed long ago. Site and temple open daily; visitors can climb to the gallery level.

Puducherry: Puducherry (Pondicherry) District Lighthouses
Founded in 1673 by the French East India Company, Puducherry (formerly Pondicherry in English or Pondichéry in French) was the chief French colonial settlement in India. After nearly three centuries of French rule it was united with the Republic of India in 1954 and has been governed as a union territory since 1956. The union territory also includes the enclaves of Karaikal in Tamil Nadu (see below for the Karaikal lighthouse), Yaman in Andhra Pradesh, and Mahé in Kerala (see the Kerala page for the Mahé lighthouse).

South Coast Lighthouses

 
The strait separating India and Sri Lanka, known in Tamil as Sethusamudram ("sea bridge"), is crossed by a chain of reefs and islands called Adam's Bridge (also called Rama's Bridge, or Rama Setu in Tamil). North of Adam's Bridge the strait is a shallow embayment called Palk Bay or Palk Strait. South of Adam's Bridge is a much deeper embayment called Mannar Bay or the Gulf of Mannar. In its natural condition Palk Bay is too shallow for modern ships, and the Adam's Bridge line of shoals is impassable for all but the smallest vessels. Since 2004 the government of India has studied the possibility of dredging a deepwater channel some 83 km (45 mi) long to open the strait to the largest ships. The Sethusamudram Shipping Canal Project has met considerable resistance from scientists and environmental groups. Considerable work has been done but as of 2020 work is stopped and it is not clear that it will be completed. However, in preparation for possible shipping increases several large new lighthouses were built along the strait.

* [Chola Lighthouse]
10th century CE. Brick ruins on the beach at Calimere Point are remnants of a lighthouse built during the reign of Paranthaka Chola (907-955 CE), one of the early rulers of the Chola dynasty that ruled south India from the mid ninth to early thirteenth centuries. Wikimedia has a photo, Jeevan Kumar has a photo, and Dhinesh Ramalingam has a street view. Endangered: Oli Mohd's June 2004 photo shows a much taller ruin but the tsunami of December 2004 destroyed much of that tower and continuing erosion has brought the surf close to the site. In 2017 officials ordered a seawall built to protect what remains but a February 2018 photo shows only inadequate efforts. The ruins are close to an observation tower built at the end of Lighthouse Road south of the modern Calimere Point lighthouse; Google's satellite view shows the observation tower. Site open.

Pudukkottai District Lighthouses
* Ammapattinam (Manamelkudi) (2?)
2005. focal plane 49 m (161 ft); two white flashes every 15 s. 45 m (148 ft) round cylindrical concrete tower with lantern and double gallery, painted with horizontal black and white bands. S. Sridhar has a 2019 photo, a 2016 photo is available, Raja Mohamed has a 2017 closeup, and Google has a street view and a satellite view. This lighthouse and similar lights at Pasipattanam and Rameshwaram were built by the slipform method to improve navigation in the Palk Strait. The immediate purpose was to help Indian fishermen stay in Indian territorial waters without straying over into Sri Lankan waters; ultimately they may guide ships through the Sethusamudram Ship Channel. An inauguration ceremony for the three lighthouses was held here on 9 October 2005. A 1909 light list records that a light was shown from the port flagstaff. Located on a point of land south of Manamelkudi, on the west side of Palk Strait about 65 km (40 mi) southwest of Calimere Point. Site open, tower closed. Thoothukudi (Tuticorin) District (Gulf of Mannar) Lighthouses
Thoothukudi (known as Tuticorin until 2018) is a major seaport and pearl fishing center; it has a population of more than 400,000.

* Vembar (Vember)
2016. Active; focal plane 30 m (98 ft); three white flashes every 10 s. 30 m (98 ft) octagonal concrete tower with lantern and gallery, painted with black and white horizontal bands. Cyril Xavier has a photo, a 2017 photo is available, Siva Sankar has a 2020 photo, Dhananjay Singh has a distant street view, and Google has a satellite view. Located just off the beach at Vembar, a fishing village at the northern border of the Thoothukudi District. Site open, tower closed. Admiralty F0737.
* Tuticorin North Range Front
Date unknown. Active; focal plane 9 m (30 ft); quick-flashing white light. 8 m (26 ft) square pyramidal skeletal tower. A photo is available and Google has a satellite view. Located at the end of a pier in Tuticorin's VOC Port, north of the main lighthouses. Probably accessible by walking the pier. Site probably open, tower closed. Admiralty F0736; NGA 27180.
* Tuticorin North Range Rear
Date unknown. Active; focal plane 13.5 m (44 ft); quick-flashing white light. Approx. 10 m (33 ft) square pyramidal skeletal tower atop a 2- or 3-story building. Google has a street view and a satellite view. Located on Beach Road near the Customs House, 259 m (850 ft) west of the front light. Site probably open, tower closed. Admiralty F0736.1; NGA 27184.
* Pandiyan Tivu (Thoothukudi, Tuticorin) (3)
1935 (station established 1845). Inactive since 1979. Approx. 22 m (72 ft) round cylindrical concrete tower painted with horizontal black and white bands. Lantern removed. Prakash Nainar has a 2017 photo, Linston Groves has a 2019 photo, a distant view is available, Forand has a historic postcard view, and Google has a good satellite view. The lighthouse is probably endangered by its abandonment. Located about 150 m (500 ft) north of the modern lighthouse. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-063.
* Pandiyan Tivu (Thoothukudi, Tuticorin) (4)
1979 (station established 1845). Active; focal plane 48 m (157 ft); white flash every 10 s. Approx. 40 m (131 ft) round cylindrical tower with lantern and double gallery, painted with black and white spiral bands. Anitto Sylvester's 2022 photo is at right, a 2025 photo is available, Satheeshkannan Satheeshkannan has a 2017 photo, a distant view is available, and Google has a good satellite view. The first light at this station was shown from a flag post; this was replaced by an 11 m (36 ft) masonry obelisk and then by the 1935 lighthouse. Located on an island (tivu) protecting the harbor of Thoothukudi (Tuticorin). The island is connected to the mainland by a causeway and the lighthouse is accessible by road. Site open, tower closed. ARLHS IND-062; Admiralty F0734; NGA 27176.

* Manapad (Manappad) Point
1888. Active; focal plane 45 m (148 ft); two white flashes every 10 s. Approx. 18 m (59 ft) round masonry tower painted with red and white spiral bands; lantern dome painted red. Kanyakumari District (Cape Comorin Area) Lighthouses
** Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin)
1971. Active; focal plane 63 m (207 ft); two white flashes every 20 s. 30 m (98 ft) square masonry tower with gallery and a small lantern, painted with red and white horizontal bands. A 2022 photo is at right, Vincent Kiran has a 2021 closeup, Krishna Kumar also has a closeup, V.C. Charuvalath has a good 2016 photo, Chandrachoodan Gopalakrishnan has a view from the sea, and Google has a satellite view and a closeup street view. In 2013 this was one of the 15 lighthouses identified for tourist development by DGLL. Kanyakumari (also spelled Kanniyakumari) is the southernmost tip of India at 8°05' N latitude. The British renamed it Cape Comorin, a name found on most foreign maps but rarely used in India. It is a very popular tourist destination, and many tourist web sites say "one can get a panoramic view" from the lighthouse. Located on the heights above the point of the cape. Site open, tower open 3 to 6 pm daily. . ARLHS IND-080; Admiralty F0726; NGA 27484.
**** Muttom Point (Muttam Point, Kadiapatam) (2)
1883 (British; station established 1875). Active; focal plane 43 m (141 ft); white flash every 5 s. 22 m (72 ft) round masonry tower with lantern and gallery, painted with black and white horizontal bands; lantern dome painted red. A rotating 2nd order Fresnel lens is in use. Mohanraj Kolathapilly has a good closeup, Wikimedia has a 2007 photo, Raja Gopal Sunkara has a 2018 photo, Chris Jehin has an aerial view, and Google has a satellite view. A surviving British lighthouse, Muttom Light can be seen from a popular beach. Early mast and post lights here were followed by a 22 m (72 ft) granite tower in 1875. In 2013 this was also one of the 15 lighthouses identified for tourist development by DGLL. A museum has opened as part of the station; a 3rd order Fresnel lens is on display. The lighthouse was a nominee for IALA Lighthouse of the Year in 2020. Located on a small rocky promontory 32 km (20 mi) west northwest of Kanyakumari and about 18 km (11 mi) southwest of the city of Nagarcoil. Accessible by road. Site open, museum and tower open daily. . ARLHS IND-103; Admiralty F0724; NGA 27472.
* Kolachel (Colachel)
Date unknown. Active; focal plane 18 m (59 ft); continuous red light. 18 m (59 ft) mast atop a 7-story white concrete tower of the Our Lady of Presentation Catholic Church. A good drone view is available, Thaya Anand has a photo, and Google has a satellite view. Located one block from the harbor at Colochel, a small fishing port about 50 km (30 mi) northwest of Kanyakumari. Site open, tower status unknown. Admiralty F0720; NGA 27476.
